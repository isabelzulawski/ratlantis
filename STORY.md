# Ratlantis: Level One story

**Spoilers ahead.** This page describes the full playable route and ending. For a spoiler-light introduction and play links, see the [README](README.md).

## Premise

A small, slightly shy but curious rat in New York City sees an advertisement for a fictional human Broadway production of *Ratatouille: The Musical*. The rat assumes that an actual rat might audition for the rat role.

The adventure follows that misunderstanding through a city that has a very different meaning at rat scale. No humans appear on screen. The rat's ambition is sincere, even when its interpretation of the human world is mistaken.

## Times Square and the search for Broadway

The player creates and names a rat, chooses its fur color, and begins exploring Times Square. The Broadway billboard gives the rat its goal. Pizza can be collected for later.

A discarded subway map points toward Broadway–Lafayette. The rat takes the name as a promising lead and heads downtown.

## Washington Square Park and The City Records

In the park, the rat asks a squirrel about Broadway. The squirrel boasts about its relationship with humans, then points out how differently rats are portrayed in the newspaper.

The player can explore three interactive newspaper spreads about NYC rodent inspection records. After the conversation, the squirrel gives the rat a copy to keep in its backpack.

This reading experience is optional. It adds a human view of rats alongside the game's rat-centered world without becoming a required lesson or quest gate.

## The subway and the accidental arrival

The route continues through Broadway–Lafayette station and a train journey. An accidental drainage descent carries the rat into the hidden city below.

A tin-can boat brings the rat to Ratlantis, where a dockworker objects to the unexpected arrival. The underground neighborhood is already busy with deliveries, a café, homes, and businesses made from discarded objects.

The dockworker directs the aspiring performer toward a retired busker in Brooklyn. Bread beside the underground café provides another food option if the player did not collect pizza.

## Brooklyn: finding a voice

The rat crosses the bridge maintenance route and finds the retired busker, who opens with “I don't perform anymore.”

Conversation reveals that he is hungry. Giving him either bread or pizza persuades him to teach. The player repeats three short note sequences, earning the singing skill.

The busker then gives the rat a ferry ticket. Its message, “Ask for the rat with the mop,” becomes the next lead. An optional pendant can also be discovered in Brooklyn.

## The ferry: finding a rhythm

Aboard the Staten Island ferry's rat-maintained work area, the rat meets a worker with a mop. He offers to demonstrate the ferry's rhythm.

The player practices and completes a forgiving movement sequence using left, right, and jump cues. Completing the activity earns dancing and opens the onward passage toward Queens.

The current ferry activity focuses on rhythm; securing sliding deck objects was an earlier design idea and is not a required task in this version.

## Queens: finding a character

An electrical-cabinet theater becomes the setting for an acting lesson. The director welcomes the rat to experiment with the costume rack.

Nine costume pieces offer headwear, clothing, and props. Outfit choice is expressive and does not determine success. The player then chooses responses for joy, sorrow, and anger in a miniature drama about a royal breadcrumb, earning acting.

The director gives directions onward through the service pipe.

## The audition-room vent

With singing, dancing, and acting learned, the rat follows the pipe into the audition building. A warm-lit grille opens onto a quiet, empty audition-room view.

The rat remains behind the vent, looking toward an empty director's chair, an illuminated audition spot, and the edge of a curtain. No humans appear.

Level One ends here. There is no playable audition or final stage performance, and the misunderstanding about the production remains unresolved.

## Narrative approach

The story uses understated humor, conversations, and small acts of help rather than combat or punishment. Its central contrast is between the rat's hopeful theatrical ambition and the practical, everyday lives of the rats it meets.

Optional exploration, accessories, the journal, and the newspaper give the player room to linger without changing the ending.
